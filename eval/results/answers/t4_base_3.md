**Short answer: the data doesn't show a clear website effect. The best estimate is about +2.5% (roughly €700 a week), and that is too small to tell apart from normal week-to-week noise.**

**Why the obvious comparison is wrong.** If you compare the 8 weeks after launch with the 8 weeks before, sales rose 30% (€23.5k to €30.5k a week). But the launch on 3 Nov fell right at the start of the holiday season. The same 8 weeks in 2024, with no redesign, rose 27% (€21.3k to €27.0k). Almost all of the 30% is the normal November–December peak, not the website.

**How I adjusted for that.** I compared each week with the same week a year earlier. The weeks line up, including Black Friday week.

| Period | Growth vs. same week last year |
|---|---|
| Jan–Oct 2025 (before launch, 43 weeks) | **+10.4%** (the normal trend) |
| 3 Nov–22 Dec 2025 (after launch, 8 weeks) | **+13.0%** |
| **Gap that might be due to the website** | **about +2.6 points** |

A second method gives the same answer: comparing the before/after jump in 2025 with the same jump in 2024 also gives about +2.3%, or €680 a week.

**What to tell the board:**
- **Estimate:** about **+2.5% in weekly sales, around €700 a week** (about €5.5k over the first 8 weeks) on top of the existing ~10% yearly growth.
- **Confidence:** low. Growth compared with the same week last year normally swings by about ±7 points from one week to the next, and we only have 8 weeks since launch. A rough 95% range runs from about −1.5% to +6.5%, so the result doesn't rule out zero effect.
- **Suggested wording:** "Sales are up 13% on last year since the relaunch, compared with 10% growth before it. The redesign may be adding around 2–3%, but it's too early to confirm. We'll check again after a full quarter outside the holiday season."

Please don't report the 30% figure. That is the holiday season, not the website. The estimate will be clearer once January–February 2026 data is in, because there's no seasonal spike then to confuse the picture. Conversion rate or traffic data from the site would also help separate the website's effect from demand.
