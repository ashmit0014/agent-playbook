I didn't find a defensible board number in this file. The headline "+30–38%" figure is almost all the usual Christmas lift. Once that is removed, the redesign shows about +2.4% (roughly €5–6k over 8 weeks), and that is the same size as normal week-to-week noise.

## Framing

- **Exact question:** How much did weekly online sales in Nov–Dec 2025 go up because of the 2025-11-03 redesign, compared with what they would have been without it?
- **What would settle it:** A comparison that holds the season and the underlying growth trend fixed, and shows a lift clearly bigger than the normal swings in that same comparison before launch.
- **Decision it feeds:** The number you give the board, and probably whether the redesign is called a success.

## Questions only you can answer (I used the defaults below; tell me if any are wrong)

1. **Is there sales data after 2025-12-22?** The file stops there, and today is 2026-11-12. *Default: no.* This matters most: 10 more months would make the answer far clearer.
2. **Did anything else change around Nov 2025?** For example promotions, ad spend, prices, a new product range, or a Black Friday campaign different from 2024. *Default: nothing different from 2024.*
3. **Is this all online sales,** with no change in how sales were tracked or attributed when the new site went live? *Default: yes, measured the same way throughout.*
4. **Was there a control?** For example a market or channel that didn't get the redesign, or an A/B test. *Default: no.*

## The answer

**Main claim (Supported):** sales rose, but the data can't separate the redesign from normal growth. My best estimate of the redesign's effect is **+2.4% (about €5.5–6k over the 8 post-launch weeks)**, within a plausible range of **about −1% to +5% (about −€1k to +€12k)**. Treat any single number in that range as fragile.

## Evidence

All numbers were recomputed from the 104 rows in the CSV: 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-22, no gaps.

| Comparison | Lift | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Post-launch weeks vs. all earlier weeks | +37.7% | Verified as arithmetic. Not a measure of the redesign: it includes Christmas and a year of growth |
| Post-launch 8 weeks vs. the 8 weeks before | +29.8% | Same problem: it compares Nov–Dec with Sep–Oct |
| Year-over-year, Nov 3–Dec 22 2025 vs. the same weeks of 2024 | +12.85% | Verified |
| Year-over-year growth before launch (Jan–Oct 2025) | +10.1% (+10.3% Sep–Oct, +11.4% October) | Verified |
| **Excess over the usual growth = redesign effect estimate** | **+2.3% to +2.5%** | Supported as an estimate; the causal reading is at most Plausible |
| 2025 holiday jump (Nov–Dec vs. Sep–Oct) compared with the 2024 jump | +2.3% | Supported. A second method gives the same size |

**Why I won't call it a real effect:**
- **The jump is inside the normal range.** Year-over-year growth over rolling 8-week windows ran from 6.3% to 13.5% during 2025 before launch. The post-launch window (12.85%) is inside that range, and below the 13.5% reached in mid-September with no redesign.
- **Fake launch dates produce similar jumps.** I pretended the launch happened at each earlier 2025 date (28 such tests) and measured the same way. Those gave −4.3% to +3.6% (typical swing 1.8 points). Against the trailing-17-week baseline, 26% of them were as large as the real one. These tests overlap, so treat them as a rough check, not a formal significance test.
- **One week does a lot of the work.** The week of Nov 17 2025 shows +23% year-over-year, but mostly because the same week in 2024 was unusually low (€22.5k).

## What the comparison assumes, and the strongest rival explanation

- **Comparison:** each 2025 week against the same weekday-aligned week of 2024 (Black Friday falls in matching weeks: Nov 25 2024 and Nov 24 2025), then subtracting the growth already happening before launch.
- **Assumes:** that without the redesign, 2025 would have kept growing at its Jan–Oct rate over 2024, and that nothing else changed in Nov–Dec 2025 compared with Nov–Dec 2024.
- **Strongest counter-case:** growth was already speeding up (11.4% in October, 13.5% in mid-September), or the 2025 holiday promotions or market were stronger. Either would explain the whole +2.4% with no help from the redesign. On the other side, the redesign's effect could be bigger than it looks if 2025 promotions were weaker than in 2024.

## Still unresolved, and what would settle it

- **Did the effect last?** Data for Jan–Oct 2026 would let you check whether a few points of extra growth persisted outside the holiday season. That is the biggest single improvement available.
- **Was it the website or something else?** Conversion rate, sessions, and average order value before and after launch would show where any change came from. A promotions and ad-spend calendar for 2024 vs. 2025 would rule rival causes in or out.
- **Is there a control group?** Any non-redesigned market, channel, or A/B holdout would allow a proper comparison.

**What I'd tell the board:** "Sales were up 12.9% year-over-year after launch, against 10% growth before it. At most a few points of that might be due to the redesign (central estimate about +2%, about €6k over 8 weeks), and we can't yet tell it apart from normal variation. We'll report again with 2026 data."
