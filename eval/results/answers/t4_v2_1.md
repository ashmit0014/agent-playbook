## What the sources say

- **NOTE.md** says the launch was 2025-11-03 and that the data is weekly online sales in euros, with weeks starting Monday. No figure in it repeats anything else, so there are no duplicated claims to trace back to one origin.
- **weekly_sales.csv** has 105 weeks, from 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-22. That gives 8 weeks after launch. It has no other variables: no traffic, marketing, prices or promotions.

## What the data shows

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Simple before vs after (8 weeks before launch vs 8 weeks after) | **+29.8%** (€23,519 → €30,523 a week) |
| The same calendar weeks in 2024, with no redesign | **+26.9%** (€21,318 → €27,047) |
| Growth vs the same week last year, Jan–Oct 2025 (before launch) | **+10.1%** (Aug–Oct: +10.7%) |
| Growth vs the same week last year, the 8 weeks after launch | **+12.9%** |
| Excess over the existing trend | **about +2 to 3%**, roughly €700 a week, about €5–6k in total |

The calendar weeks line up between the two years, so Black Friday and Christmas fall in the same rows each year.

**Why the 30% figure is wrong:** sales jump every November and December. In 2024 they rose 27% over the same weeks with no redesign. On top of that, the business was already growing about 10% a year before the launch. Together, those two effects explain nearly all of the 30%.

**Why the remaining 2–3% isn't solid evidence:** before launch, the growth vs last year over any 8-week stretch ranged from **6.3% to 13.6%**. The 12.9% after launch sits inside that range. Measured against that range, the website's effect could be anything from about **−1% to +6%**.

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** the "~30% increase" is mostly seasonality and existing growth, not the website. The 2024 data shows the same jump directly.
- **Low confidence:** whether any real lift exists. The best estimate is about +2–3%, but the data can't tell it apart from zero.
- **What I couldn't check:**
  - There are only 8 weeks after launch, all of them in the holiday peak.
  - I can't rule out other causes, such as changes to marketing, promotions, pricing or product range in Q4 2025, or a different Black Friday performance.
  - There are no traffic or conversion figures and no comparison group, such as an A/B test or a channel the redesign didn't touch.
  - I took the data at face value and couldn't verify it against the source system.

## Suggested wording for the board

"Sales rose about 30% after launch, but nearly all of that is the usual holiday season plus our existing ~10% annual growth. After adjusting for both, the redesign's contribution is roughly 0–5% and not yet distinguishable from normal variation."

Two things would give a real number: January–February 2026 data, when there's no holiday peak to distort the comparison, and the site's conversion-rate figures, ideally from an A/B test.
