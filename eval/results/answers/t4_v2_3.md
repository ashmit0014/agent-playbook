I've finished the analysis. The short answer for the board: **the data can't support a confident number for the website's effect.** The best estimate is about **+3% (roughly €7k over the 8 weeks after launch)**, and the plausible range runs from **about 0% to +6%**. Please don't present the headline "+30%". Almost all of it is the normal Christmas peak.

**Sources.** There is one source, `weekly_sales.csv`: 104 weekly totals from 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-22. `NOTE.md` only repeats the launch date and says the figures are weekly online sales in euros. Nothing else in the folder confirms or contradicts the data, so I couldn't check where the numbers come from, whether they include VAT or refunds, or whether the definition changed when the new site launched.

**1. The naive figure of about +30% is wrong as an effect.**
- What the data says: the 8 weeks after launch averaged €30.5k a week. The 8 weeks before averaged €23.5k. That's +30%.
- Why it's misleading: the launch week (3 Nov) is exactly when sales rise every year for the holidays. The same 8-week jump in 2024, with no redesign, was about **+26%** (around €21.4k a week up to about €27.0k a week).
- Confidence: high that most of the +30% is seasonal.

**2. Comparing each week with the same week a year earlier gives about +3%, but that could be noise.**
- From January to October 2025, before the launch, sales ran about **10–11% above the same weeks of 2024**. The average over 8-week windows was +9.7%, and individual windows ranged from +6.3% to +13.5%.
- In the 8 weeks after launch, sales were **+12.9%** above the same weeks of 2024 (€244.2k vs €216.4k).
- The extra growth above the normal trend is about 3 percentage points, or roughly **€6.8k over 8 weeks (about €850 a week)**. Depending on which baseline you pick, it ranges from about €14k to slightly negative.
- How unusual is +12.9%? One of the 36 pre-launch 8-week windows was higher (+13.5%). Week-to-week growth varies a lot: a typical week is about 7 points above or below the average.
- Confidence: low to moderate. The estimate points slightly upward, but it is within normal variation.

**3. What I couldn't rule out.** None of these can be checked from this file:
- Other things that changed in November–December 2025: promotions, ad spend, prices, stock, competitors, or how strong the season was overall.
- Timing differences between the two years. Black Friday fell in the same week in both years, so that one is handled.
- Only 8 weeks after launch, all in peak season. There's no data from January 2026 onward, even though today is November 2026.
- No comparison group, such as a channel or region that didn't get the new site.

**What I'd tell the board:** "Sales after launch were about 13% above last year, compared with about 10% growth earlier in the year. If the redesign caused the gap, it's worth roughly 3% (about €7k over the first 8 weeks). But that is within normal variation, and we haven't separated it from other holiday factors."

**How to firm it up:**
- Extend the same year-over-year comparison to the ~10 months of 2026 sales that should exist now. More weeks outside the holiday season would make the estimate much tighter.
- Pull the marketing and promotions calendar for November–December 2025 and 2024.
- If possible, use conversion rate per session from web analytics, which isolates the site itself better than total sales.
