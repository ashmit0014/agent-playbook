**Why**

1. **The "40% productivity" figure doesn't hold up.** The news weekly, the blog and the HR magazine all quote it, but it comes from a single press release. That release covered one office in Japan, for one month (August 2019), with no comparison group, using the company's own figures. It also measured sales per employee, not productivity. The academic review says the figure "should not be generalised."
2. **The better evidence is real but narrower.**
   - Wellbeing gains show up consistently. In the UK pilot, 71% of respondents reported less burnout.
   - Effects on output are mixed. The UK pilot didn't measure productivity directly. Only 23 of 61 firms gave revenue data, and those firms averaged a 1.4% rise. The firms also chose to take part, so the results may not apply to everyone.
   - 56 of the 61 firms carried on with the four-day week, but only 18 made it permanent.
3. **Most of our staff are in the hard case.** The academic review found that in customer-facing, fixed-hours work, coverage fell unless staff were added. That describes 25 of our 40 people. The HR magazine reports a logistics firm that dropped its trial after six weeks because complaints rose.

**What it means for the support centre**

| Option | Weekly staff hours | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Today: 25 people × 40 h | 1,000 | 100% |
| 32-hour week, days off staggered | 800 | 80% (about 5 fewer people on the phones each day) |
| 32-hour week plus new hires | 1,000 | 100%, but needs about 6 more agents (roughly 25% more support payroll) |
| Four 10-hour days, staggered | 1,000 | 100% at no extra cost (long days on the phones, so make it voluntary) |

**What to ask leadership to approve**

- **Developer pilot:** six months, 32 hours at full pay.
  - Record 2–3 months of current numbers first: work delivered, cycle time, how fast support escalations get answered, sick days and a wellbeing survey.
  - Agree in advance what counts as success and what would make you stop early.
  - Stagger days off so a developer is always available for urgent support issues.
- **Support centre:** at the same time, a voluntary trial of four 10-hour days. Running both together avoids a two-tier company where only developers get a shorter week.
- **Permanent decision:** make it at the six-month review, based on your own numbers.

**Limits:** all the sources are short summaries, and none of them studies a company of your size with this mix of support and engineering staff. That's why the pilot data should decide the final policy. I also gave the WorkSmarter blog little weight because it's a promotional list with no sources.
