# t4_v2_2

cost_usd: 0.259  turns: 5

**Short answer: the data doesn't show that the new website increased sales. The best estimate of any effect is about +2% (roughly €5k over 8 weeks), and that is within the range of normal week-to-week noise. I wouldn't give the board a positive number. "No measurable effect so far, at most a few percent" is what the data supports.**

## What the source says
I used two files. `weekly_sales.csv` has 104 weeks of weekly online sales in euros, from 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-22. `NOTE.md` says only when the site launched and what the units are. There is nothing else: no second source, no summaries and no marketing or traffic data. So every figure below comes straight from that one CSV.

## Why the obvious number is misleading
- **Simple before vs. after (the number to avoid):** average weekly sales were €23,519 in the 8 weeks before launch and €30,523 in the 8 weeks after. That's **+30%**, and against the whole pre-launch period it's +38%.
- **But the same jump happened last year with no new site.** Nov–Dec 2024 rose **+27%** over the preceding weeks. The launch fell right at the start of the holiday season, so most of that +30% is seasonality, not the website.
- **The business was also already growing.** In every pre-launch week of 2025, sales ran about **10–11% above** the same week of 2024. The rate was steady: 11.4% average for Jan–Oct, 10.3% over the last 26 weeks, 10.8% over the last 13.

## A fairer comparison: this year vs. last year, same weeks
- After launch, the 8 weeks ran **12.9% above** the same 8 weeks of 2024 (€244,187 vs. €216,378). The Black Friday weeks line up in both years.
- The pre-launch trend was +10.3% to +11.4%. Applying it to last year's holiday weeks gives an expected €238.6k–€239.8k.
- **The extra over that expectation is about €4.4k–€5.6k, or +1.8% to +2.4%.**

## Is that +2% real?
I ran a placebo test: the same calculation at fake "launch dates" every week from July to October 2025, when nothing launched.
- Those fake dates produced apparent effects from **−1.5% to +4.1%**.
- The real launch, at +2.5% on that method, sits inside that range. It is no bigger than what normal fluctuation produces.
- A rough statistical range for the effect is about **−2% to +7%**, which includes zero.

## How confident I am
| Conclusion | Confidence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The +30% before/after jump is mostly seasonal, not the website | **High** | The same pattern (+27%) happened in 2024 with no redesign |
| The website's effect, if any, is small (a few percent at most) | **Moderate** | Sales against last year rose only ~2 points above an already steady trend |
| The website increased sales at all | **Can't be determined** | The estimate isn't distinguishable from noise on placebo dates |

## What I couldn't check
- **Only one prior year** to set the seasonal baseline. If the 2025 holiday season was stronger for other reasons (prices, promotions, ad spend, competitors, the economy), that would show up as a "website effect". The reverse is possible too.
- **Nothing else that might have changed in Nov 2025.** The file has no marketing, pricing, traffic, conversion or channel data.
- **No comparison group**, such as offline sales, another region, or a holdout that kept the old site. Any of these would be much stronger evidence.
- **Only 8 weeks after launch, all in the holidays.** The January–February 2026 numbers, compared with the same weeks of 2025, would be a cleaner test.
- **Unknown data quality.** I can't confirm how "sales" is defined, whether it includes returns, or whether it was measured the same way before and after the redesign. A new site can change tracking.

**Suggested line for the board:** "Holiday sales grew about 13% year over year, in line with the roughly 10–11% growth we had before the redesign. So far there's no measurable sales lift from the new website, at most a few percent, which we can't yet tell apart from noise. We'll re-measure with Q1 2026 data."
