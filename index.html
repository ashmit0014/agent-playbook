Say what you need. Add only what helps.

This page has no AI in it. It helps you write the message you paste into an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Write your request, tick what applies, and copy the result.

Most of the time the result is just your request: in our tests, a clear request worked as well as a long list of rules, and often better. The exception was work spread over several conversations, so that is the one case where the page adds something.

1 What do you want the AI to do?

Write it as you would to a capable colleague: what you want, what a good result looks like, and anything to avoid. Mention any documents it should use, and attach them when you paste. If you want to see a sample before it does everything, say so.

Writing: "Write a one-page cover letter for the attached job ad, using my attached CV. Friendly but professional." Building: "Make a spreadsheet that totals my spending by category each month. I will paste a few sample rows with the account number removed."

2 Does any of this apply?

Tick only what is true. If nothing is, leave them all empty.

Situations

3 Copy it into your AI tool

Where do I paste it, and what happens next?

In a chat app (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini): the easiest start, and enough for most things, including files it makes for you to download. Start a new chat, paste, attach any documents your request mentions, and send. It cannot change anything on your computer, and a new chat remembers nothing from the last one.

In a tool that works on your files (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor): it saves and changes files in a folder for you, which helps when work runs over several days. These must be installed, may need a paid plan, and some are started by typing their name in a terminal window. Open the tool in a folder made for the project and paste into the box where you type to it. It asks before running commands; if you do not understand a request, do not approve it yet: ask it to explain in plain words what it will do. If the files matter, make a copy of the folder first.

Privacy: what you paste or attach is sent to the company that runs the AI. Remove account numbers, passwords and other private details first, or try it with a small made-up sample.

Coming back later: in a chat app, go back to the same chat. In a tool that works on your files, open it in the same folder and say what you want next, for example "Continue the budget spreadsheet: add a chart of monthly totals." If you ticked "The work will continue in another conversation" last time, the AI left notes in the folder and will find them. Tick it again if there will be more conversations after this one.

What this page does not do

It installs nothing and changes no settings. It cannot see or change the rules your AI tool already follows; it only adds the text you see above. Written instructions guide the AI but do not enforce anything: for limits that must hold, use backups, version control or your tool's permission settings.

What this is

Agent Playbook started as a large set of rules drawn from real projects where AI agents went wrong (version 1). Version 2 tested those rules against a plain, clear request. Only what changed the outcome is offered here; the rest became notes for people. The evaluation shows what helped, what made no difference, and what it cost. Tested with one model (Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code); other tools may behave differently.
